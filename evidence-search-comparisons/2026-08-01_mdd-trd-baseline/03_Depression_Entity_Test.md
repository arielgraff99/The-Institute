# Search 3: Is treatment-resistant depression distinct from major depression?

## Bottom line

Current evidence supports treatment-resistant depression as a clinically important, regulatory-recognized and high-burden treatment-course phenotype. It does not yet establish TRD as one biologically distinct disease separate from MDD.

The best-fitting model is heterogeneous enrichment: after successive treatment failures, the selected population becomes enriched for severe/chronic depression, anxiety and insomnia, bipolar-spectrum or psychotic features, medical comorbidity, inflammatory/metabolic burden, pharmacokinetic and adherence problems, social adversity and possibly several neurobiological endotypes. Some patients may have a distinct immune, metabolic, circuit or molecular endotype; others have a later stage of depressive illness; others have pseudo-resistance. The current binary label combines them.

## Competing hypotheses

| Hypothesis | Prediction | Current assessment |
|---|---|---|
| H1: TRD is a distinct disease | Stable baseline discriminator separates future TRD from responsive MDD across settings and mechanisms | Not demonstrated |
| H2: TRD is a later or more severe stage of MDD | Risk rises continuously with chronicity, recurrence, severity and failed interventions | Substantially supported, but not sufficient for every case |
| H3: TRD contains multiple biological endotypes | Different biomarker/circuit subgroups predict different treatments | Plausible and increasingly supported; not yet clinically validated as a complete taxonomy |
| H4: TRD is an operational residual category | Membership depends partly on definition, treatment choice, adherence, dose/duration and access | Strongly supported |
| H5: Mixed model | Stage, endotype and pseudo-resistance contribute in different proportions | Best overall fit |

## Domain-by-domain application of historical splitting criteria

### 1. Definition and boundary

The modal definition is inadequate response to at least two adequate antidepressant trials, but reviews find substantial variation in number and type of treatments, dose, duration, adherence verification and response threshold. This makes the boundary treatment-dependent and partly circular. TRD can also disappear when the underlying diagnosis, adherence, dosing, comorbidity or treatment selection is corrected.

Judgment: strong clinical utility; weak ontologic independence.

Key evidence: Gaynes et al. found that two adequate failures is the most common definition, not a universally standardized construct. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31638723/). The European consensus framework distinguishes response thresholds and persistent versus treatment-resistant depression, illustrating ongoing definitional revision. [Molecular Psychiatry](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41380-021-01381-x).

### 2. Clinical phenotype and longitudinal course

Across the systematic review of 57 studies, the most repeated predictors were greater severity, suicidality, recurrence, longer illness or episode duration, anxiety and insomnia. Cardiovascular disease, pain and thyroid dysfunction also recur. These findings favor severity/chronicity and comorbidity enrichment, not a clean novel syndrome. Sex results were contradictory, and many associations were retrospective.

Judgment: moderate evidence for a stage/enrichment model; low specificity for a separate disease.

Source: O’Connor et al. systematic review. [Journal of Clinical Psychiatry](https://www.psychiatrist.com/jcp/predictors-risk-factors-treatment-resistant-depression-systematic-review/).

### 3. Genetics

The 2025 Nordic GWAS/meta-analysis included about 84,000 MDD cases and 2,062 severe TRD cases. It reported one genome-wide significant TRD-risk locus, larger rare copy-number variants, and higher psychiatric polygenic burden. However, genetic correlation was very high with MDD (rg 0.90) and bipolar disorder (rg 0.86); treatment-resistance heritability within MDD was imprecise, and definitions materially affected estimates. This supports greater inherited liability and cross-disorder burden, not genetic discontinuity.

Judgment: moderate evidence of quantitative genetic enrichment; low evidence of a categorical boundary.

Source: Xiong et al. [Molecular Psychiatry](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41380-025-03084-z).

### 4. Transcriptomics and epigenetics

A 2025 blood transcriptomic study reported thousands of differentially expressed genes and immune/epigenetic signatures in TRD compared with non-resistant MDD. This is important hypothesis-generating evidence. At present, the result requires independent prospective replication, careful control of medication exposure, illness duration, cell composition and batch effects, and testing of predictive performance before treatment.

Judgment: promising but currently insufficient for disease splitting.

Source: Sirés et al. [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40930269/).

### 5. Inflammation and immune biology

TRD cohorts often show higher CRP, TNF-related or IL-6-related signals, and inflammation may predict poorer response to conventional antidepressants in a subset. Yet inflammatory elevation is neither universal in TRD nor specific to it; obesity, pain, medical illness, stress and treatment exposure confound associations. Individual biomarker thresholds have not become validated companion diagnostics.

Judgment: credible endotype candidate; not a universal TRD mechanism.

Sources: Strawbridge et al. meta-analysis of inflammation and treatment response. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26169573/); Chamberlain et al. CRP in TRD. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6124647/); Almutabagani et al. review. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9844360/).

### 6. Neuroimaging and circuits

Systematic reviews report structural and functional differences involving frontolimbic, default-mode, salience, reward and related networks. However, studies use varying TRD definitions, medication states, acquisition methods and analytic pipelines; coordinate-based synthesis is exploratory and individual-level clinical classification is not validated.

Judgment: evidence of circuit heterogeneity and severity effects; insufficient specificity and transportability.

Sources: Miola et al. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36641802/); Runia et al. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34890601/).

### 7. Proteomics and metabolomics

MDD studies identify immune, lipid, energy-metabolism, oxidative-stress and neuroplasticity signals, but even broad depression reviews find limited replication across platforms. TRD-specific hypothesis-free omics literature is smaller and frequently cross-sectional. Medication, diet, BMI, sleep, smoking and medical illness are major alternative explanations.

Judgment: exploratory endotype evidence, presently low taxonomic value.

Sources: Costa et al. MDD metabolomics review. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8993993/); Pu et al. large-scale depression metabolomic/proteomic consistency analysis. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39955468/).

### 8. Treatment response

ECT, rTMS, ketamine/esketamine and augmentation strategies can benefit patients meeting TRD criteria, and the FDA recognizes TRD as an indication population for esketamine. But response to a treatment with a novel mechanism does not demonstrate a separate disease: responsive and non-TRD MDD may also respond, and TRD remains heterogeneous in response to all these interventions.

Judgment: strong evidence for a distinct treatment pathway; weak evidence that treatment response defines one pathology.

Sources: current FDA esketamine label. [FDA](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2025/211243s016lbl.pdf); FDA original review defining TRD operationally after at least two adequate oral antidepressants. [FDA](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2019/211243Orig1s000RiskR.pdf).

### 9. Social and socioeconomic evidence

Lower income, unemployment, reduced access, single status and other adverse social conditions appear associated with TRD in some cohorts, while social isolation and loneliness are undermeasured. These factors can affect incidence, chronic stress, adherence, treatment availability and the probability of being labeled resistant. They are part of causal context and also potential classification bias.

Judgment: meaningful contributors and confounders; inadequate prospective mechanistic separation.

Sources: Forthman et al. sociodemographic study. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40645528/); Baig-Ward et al. societal burden review. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11008705/).

### 10. Burden, mortality and health-service use

TRD is associated with higher health-care utilization, disability, cost and mortality than non-TRD MDD. This validates clinical importance. It does not independently establish distinct etiology because selection by severity, chronicity and comorbidity produces the same prediction.

Judgment: high confidence in burden difference; low specificity for ontology.

Source: Nordic genetic paper’s linked population evidence and references. [Molecular Psychiatry](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41380-025-03084-z); Shah et al. economic burden. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8425301/).

## Scorecard against successful historical splits

| Criterion | TRD status | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline definition independent of treatment outcome | Not met | High |
| Reproducible categorical biomarker | Not met | High |
| Distinct cause or pathology across most cases | Not met | Moderate-high |
| Different prospective natural history beyond baseline severity | Partially met | Moderate |
| Independent replication across diverse cohorts | Partially met for clinical burden; weak for biomarkers | Moderate |
| Prospective treatment-by-biomarker interaction | Rare/not established for a general TRD boundary | High |
| Different treatment pathway | Met | High |
| Regulatory recognition | Met as an indication population | High |
| Separate diagnostic ontology | Not established | High |
| Mixed/indeterminate cases explicitly modeled | Usually not met by binary definitions | High |

## Explanatory model

The evidence supports the following causal structure as an inference, not a demonstrated single pathway:

Predisposing liability and depressive endotype + episode severity/chronicity + comorbid psychiatric/medical disease + social conditions/access + treatment selection/exposure/adherence → probability of sequential nonresponse → TRD label → further illness and treatment effects that alter biomarkers and function.

This structure explains why cross-sectional TRD biomarkers may be causes, consequences, correlates of severity, effects of medication, or mixtures of all four.

## Research design capable of deciding the question

1. Enroll treatment-naive first-episode MDD before the future TRD label exists.
2. Obtain harmonized multi-omic, immune, metabolic, endocrine, imaging, cognitive, sleep, digital and socioeconomic measurements.
3. Establish diagnosis, bipolarity, psychosis, substance use, medical mimics, pharmacokinetics and adherence prospectively.
4. Use measurement-based, algorithmically adequate treatments while allowing pragmatic randomization across mechanisms.
5. Define resistance continuously and categorically; include psychotherapy and neuromodulation in sensitivity analyses.
6. Learn candidate subtypes only in training cohorts and freeze them before external validation.
7. Require temporal stability, replication, calibration and incremental decision utility.
8. Test subgroup-by-treatment interactions, not merely within-subgroup response.
9. Model stage transitions and permit mixed/indeterminate membership.
10. Determine whether a baseline subtype predicts both natural history and differential benefit strongly enough to change care.

If successful, this study may not divide depression into only MDD and TRD. It may instead identify several mechanistic depression endotypes that cut across current response-based labels.

## Final determination

- Directly demonstrated: TRD is more burdensome and has a distinct later-line treatment/regulatory pathway.
- Supported by convergent evidence: TRD populations are enriched for severity, chronicity, recurrence, suicidality, anxiety, insomnia and medical comorbidity.
- Mechanistically plausible: immune, metabolic, circuit and molecular endotypes contribute to resistance in subsets.
- Exploratory: a blood transcriptomic subtype or genomic profile defines a stable, general TRD entity.
- Not supported at present: all TRD is one distinct disease biologically separate from MDD.

The most defensible nomenclature today is “treatment-course specifier or difficult-to-treat depressive state containing multiple possible endotypes and stages,” while preserving TRD for regulatory and service purposes.
