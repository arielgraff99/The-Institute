# Model Comparison: Pretreatment Inflammation and Antidepressant Resistance

## Directly demonstrated findings

### 1. Pretreatment inflammatory markers can be associated with differential antidepressant outcome

GENDEP randomized patients to escitalopram or nortriptyline and found a baseline CRP-by-drug interaction, with low CRP favoring escitalopram and higher CRP favoring nortriptyline. This is direct evidence of possible treatment moderation, but it does not demonstrate a separate TRD disease entity.

Source: Uher et al., Am J Psychiatry 2014. DOI: 10.1176/appi.ajp.2014.14010094.

CO-MED secondary analyses similarly reported treatment-moderating inflammatory signals, but these were post hoc/secondary analyses of one parent trial and should be treated as one study family rather than multiple independent replications.

Sources: Jha et al., Psychoneuroendocrinology 2017, PMID 28187400; Gadad et al., J Psychiatr Res 2017, PMID 28628884; later CO-MED immunomarker analyses.

### 2. TRD groups can have higher peripheral inflammation than comparison groups

Chamberlain et al. found BMI-corrected CRP significantly elevated in a treatment-resistant MDD group compared with healthy controls. CRP also tracked vegetative symptoms, BMI, anxiety, and childhood-adversity-related measures. Because resistance had already occurred, this is cross-sectional evidence of enrichment, not proof that inflammation caused or prospectively defined resistance.

Source: Chamberlain et al., Br J Psychiatry 2019. PMID 29764522; DOI 10.1192/bjp.2018.66.

### 3. Inflammatory status may modify response to immune-targeting treatment

The infliximab TRD RCT found no overall antidepressant advantage over placebo, but a significant treatment-by-time-by-baseline-CRP interaction; high-CRP subgroup response differences were exploratory and not statistically significant as simple response-rate comparisons.

Source: Raison et al., JAMA Psychiatry 2013. PMID 22945416; ClinicalTrials.gov NCT00463580.

The 2026 Insight tocilizumab trial enrolled difficult-to-treat depression with repeated elevated hs-CRP. It was small (n=30) and not powered for definitive efficacy. The primary outcome was not statistically significant; exploratory symptom and remission patterns favored tocilizumab and baseline hs-CRP tracked improvement better than IL-6. This is useful mechanistic evidence but not confirmatory proof of a stable inflammatory TRD subtype.

Source: Foley et al., JAMA Psychiatry, published May 20, 2026. DOI 10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2026.1053; ISRCTN16942542.

## Convergent but nondecisive findings

- The 2015 Strawbridge meta-analysis found treatment nonresponders tended to have higher baseline inflammation and persistent TNF-alpha elevation, but the underlying studies were heterogeneous and predated current biomarker-validation standards.
- A 2019 systematic review of inflammatory markers in TRD found IL-6 and CRP/hs-CRP promising for predicting some treatment outcomes, especially anti-inflammatory or ketamine-related interventions, but only 10 studies were eligible and definitions/methods varied considerably.
- EMBARC, a randomized sertraline-versus-placebo biosignature study, found baseline CRP associations with outcome that were not uniform across the full sample and appeared sex-dependent in one analysis.
- A 2025 prospective Qatar cohort found standard inflammatory indices including CRP did not predict treatment response, providing direct contradictory evidence against a universal inflammation predictor.
- A 2019 Chinese prospective study reported baseline inflammatory factors did not predict antidepressant response, while another biomarker did.

## Findings weakened by integrity concerns

### 2026 Hummad meta-analysis

The review reported elevated baseline inflammatory markers associated with antidepressant nonresponse (pooled OR 2.11, 95% CI 1.84-2.43; I2 15%) and CRP subgroup OR 2.47. The paper itself acknowledges variable thresholds and inconsistent adjustment for obesity, smoking, and metabolic disease, and states that no prospective biomarker-guided treatment trials were found.

Institute audit concerns:

1. The review protocol was not prospectively registered in PROSPERO.
2. Table 1 includes Uher et al. and two Cattaneo analyses from GENDEP. These are not independent study families.
3. It includes Raison et al.'s infliximab trial, which addresses treatment-by-inflammation interaction in already-resistant patients rather than simply predicting standard antidepressant nonresponse.
4. It includes cross-sectional TRD-versus-responder evidence, which cannot establish pretreatment temporality.
5. Biomarker cutoffs range substantially and confounders were not consistently adjusted.
6. The pooled estimate therefore mixes different causal questions and requires study-family-level reconstruction before use as confirmatory evidence.

Applicable Institute rules: CB2, CB3, CB4 and possibly CB6 depending on study-specific threshold derivation.

## Shared discriminator questions

| Discriminator | Pilot judgment |
|---|---|
| Was the feature measured before resistance developed? | Usually no. Often before a specific treatment, but after prior illness/treatment exposure. |
| Does it prospectively predict resistance? | Partially for nonresponse to a treatment; not validated for future multi-trial TRD. |
| Is it stable across time and treatment? | Not established. Inflammatory markers change with illness, treatment and medical/metabolic state. |
| Specific beyond severity/chronicity/comorbidity/exposure/access? | No. Important residual confounding remains. |
| Reproduced in independent cohorts? | Mixed. Positive findings exist, but effects vary by cohort, sex, marker and treatment; companion publications reduce apparent independence. |
| Different natural history? | Not established from inflammation alone. |
| Modifies comparative treatment benefit? | Yes, promising signals in GENDEP, CO-MED and immune-targeting trials, but not yet clinically validated. |
| Would biomarker use improve decisions? | Not demonstrated prospectively. |

## Model-by-model adjudication

### H1: Distinct disease

Status: NOT DEMONSTRATED.

Reason: No identified evidence establishes a stable pretreatment inflammatory discriminator that precedes resistance, predicts future failure of at least two adequate treatments, replicates independently, and remains specific after major confounding. Cross-sectional enrichment in established TRD is insufficient.

### H2: Stage or severity

Status: PLAUSIBLE / PARTIALLY SUPPORTED.

Reason: Inflammation covaries with severity, BMI, vegetative symptoms, chronicity-related features and accumulated treatment failure. This model explains part of the TRD inflammatory signal without requiring a categorical disease boundary.

### H3: Multiple endotypes

Status: BEST SUPPORTED BIOLOGICAL MODEL IN THIS BRANCH.

Reason: Treatment-interaction signals suggest that an inflammation-linked subgroup may respond differently to serotonergic/noradrenergic treatment or immune-targeting interventions. Effects are not universal and may cut across the TRD/non-TRD boundary.

### H4: Operational treatment-course category

Status: STRONGLY SUPPORTED.

Reason: TRD status is assigned after treatment failures, and inflammatory studies use heterogeneous definitions, treatment histories, adequacy criteria and thresholds. The category is clinically useful but not biologically homogeneous.

### H5: Mixed model

Status: PREFERRED OVERALL FORMULATION.

Reason: Current findings are best explained by a mixture of biological endotypes, severity/stage effects, prior treatment exposure, comorbidity and operational classification.

### H0: Artefact or selection model

Status: MATERIAL CONTRIBUTOR REMAINS PLAUSIBLE.

Reason: BMI, smoking, metabolic disease, medical comorbidity, symptom profile, sex, treatment exposure, selective subgroup analyses, threshold heterogeneity and study-family duplication can all inflate apparent separation.

## Blocked claims

### CLAIM_BLOCKED

Claim: "Elevated CRP prospectively identifies a biologically distinct TRD disease."

Reason: No validated pretreatment multi-trial prospective classifier found; temporality and specificity criteria are unmet.

Missing evidence: treatment-naive first-episode cohort followed prospectively through standardized adequate treatment steps with external validation.

Required action: targeted prospective cohort/registry search and independent replication analysis.

### CLAIM_BLOCKED

Claim: "The 2026 pooled OR of 2.11 is independent confirmatory evidence that inflammation causes antidepressant resistance."

Reason: pooled evidence combines heterogeneous causal questions and contains overlapping study families; confounding remains substantial.

Missing evidence: study-family-deduplicated re-extraction, source-level effect verification, threshold provenance and confounder-adjusted sensitivity analyses.

Required action: reconstruct the meta-analysis ledger at study-family level before using the pooled estimate for model adjudication.

## Preferred formulation after this pilot

Inflammation is a credible candidate endotype/modifier within depressive illness and may contribute to resistance in a subset of patients. The present evidence does not justify defining TRD as a single inflammatory disease separate from MDD.

## Decisive future study

Enroll treatment-naive first-episode MDD before resistance exists; measure hs-CRP and cytokines repeatedly; predefine inflammatory thresholds; rigorously measure BMI, smoking, metabolic and inflammatory disease, sleep, severity, bipolarity, trauma, socioeconomic access and medication exposure; use standardized adequate sequential treatments with adherence/TDM verification; externally validate prediction of at least two treatment failures; then conduct a biomarker-stratified randomized trial to establish treatment-by-biomarker interaction and decision utility.
