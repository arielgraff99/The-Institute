# Dual-Track Sensitivity Result

Pilot: Pretreatment inflammation and future antidepressant resistance

Protocol extension: `config/dual_track_sensitivity_addendum.json`

## Track A: All relevant evidence, unrestricted discovery

This track retains all scientifically relevant studies regardless of registration or preregistration status and does not exclude retrospective, cross-sectional, secondary, post hoc, historical, preprint, conference, or other nonconfirmatory evidence solely on design-label grounds. Quality limitations remain visible.

### Result

The unrestricted literature produces a stronger apparent signal that inflammation is related to antidepressant resistance and treatment outcome. Positive evidence includes:

- cross-sectional enrichment of CRP/inflammatory markers in established TRD;
- prospective pretreatment biomarker associations with response to individual antidepressants;
- treatment-by-inflammatory-status interactions in GENDEP and CO-MED analyses;
- inflammatory subgroup signals in immune-targeting trials;
- systematic reviews and meta-analyses reporting higher inflammatory burden or poorer antidepressant response among patients with higher baseline inflammation;
- mechanistic and secondary analyses identifying cytokine, immune, metabolic, or symptom-pattern subgroups.

Under this inclusive view, an inflammation-associated depression subtype or endotype is strongly plausible and may contribute to treatment resistance in a clinically meaningful subset.

### Unrestricted model ordering

1. H5 Mixed model
2. H3 Multiple endotypes
3. H2 Stage or severity
4. H4 Operational treatment-course category
5. H1 Distinct disease
6. H0 Artefact or selection

This ordering reflects breadth of signal, not proof strength. H1 receives more support here than in the confirmatory track because cross-sectional and nonindependently replicated biological separation studies remain visible.

## Track B: Higher-confidence confirmatory adjudication

This track is derived from the same evidence ledger. No study is removed merely because it lacks registration. Instead, claims are promoted only when design, temporality, study-family independence, confounding control, and replication are adequate for the specific claim.

### Result

The strongest defensible conclusion becomes narrower. Pretreatment inflammatory status can moderate response to some treatments and is associated with treatment outcome in several cohorts, but current evidence does not establish that inflammation prospectively defines a separate TRD disease.

Key reasons:

- many TRD biomarker studies are cross-sectional after resistance has already occurred;
- pretreatment often means before the current treatment, not before the development of resistance;
- GENDEP and CO-MED each generate multiple publications that must be collapsed to parent study families;
- inflammatory thresholds and confounder adjustment vary;
- positive treatment-interaction findings are not universally reproduced;
- no identified independently validated classifier prospectively predicts future failure of at least two adequate treatments from treatment-naive or early-course MDD.

### Confirmatory model ordering

1. H5 Mixed model
2. H3 Multiple endotypes
3. H4 Operational treatment-course category
4. H2 Stage or severity
5. H0 Artefact or selection
6. H1 Distinct disease

## Delta analysis

| Is inflammation relevant to antidepressant resistance? | Yes, strong convergent signal | Yes, but heterogeneous and treatment-dependent | Stable |

## Studies without registration

Lack of registration is not an exclusion criterion in either track.

An unregistered study can therefore:

- remain fully visible in Track A;
- contribute to Track B if the claim is appropriate to its design and other validity requirements are met;
- be downgraded for selective-reporting uncertainty when registration would have been expected;
- remain important historical or mechanistic evidence even when it cannot establish a confirmatory causal claim.

The 2026 Hummad meta-analysis is an example. Its lack of prospective PROSPERO registration does not remove it. Its confirmatory weight is reduced primarily because of heterogeneous causal questions, overlapping GENDEP study families, threshold variation, and confounding—not registration status alone.

## Interpretation

The two-track approach prevents two opposite errors:

1. **Overrestriction:** losing important signals because an older, exploratory, unregistered, or unconventional study fails modern confirmatory standards.
2. **Overinterpretation:** allowing numerous exploratory or overlapping publications to create the appearance of independent confirmation.

A finding that exists only in Track A is retained as `CONFIRMATION_PENDING`, not discarded. A central conclusion that changes between tracks is labeled `RESTRICTION_SENSITIVE`, with the studies responsible for the change identified.

## Pilot conclusion after dual-track analysis

The inflammation signal survives restriction, so it is unlikely to be merely an artefact of registration filtering. What does not survive is the stronger ontology claim that inflammation establishes TRD as a separate disease. The more stable interpretation across both tracks is that inflammation may identify one or more treatment-relevant depressive endotypes that cut across the conventional MDD/TRD boundary.
