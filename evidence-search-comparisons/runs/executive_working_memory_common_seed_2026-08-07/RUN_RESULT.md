# Institute Serial Evidence-Family Run

Date: 2026-08-07

## Research target

Identify the most granular transdiagnostic phenotype linking cognitive decline across disorders, then test whether insulin/IGF signaling is a primary trophic driver, a context-dependent regulator/amplifier, an adaptive response, or an epiphenomenon.

The run deliberately moved away from disease labels and from global cognition. Each evidence family inherited the surviving hypotheses and unresolved discriminators from the preceding family.

## Final granular seed

### Operational seed

**Stability/fidelity of a task-relevant neural representation across load, delay, and interference in PFC-centered executive networks.**

This is more granular and more translatable than:

- dementia diagnosis;
- global cognitive decline;
- executive function as a broad syndrome;
- working memory as a task label;
- persistent firing as a single implementation;
- insulin resistance as a molecular label.

The seed is an operation that can be measured across humans, nonhuman primates, rodents, circuits, tissue, cells, organoids, and computational models.

## Why persistent firing was not retained as the seed

Persistent delay activity is important but is not universal enough to define the phenotype. Modern primate work shows that mnemonic information can be maintained in distributed and fluctuating population states rather than by one stationary firing pattern. Schizophrenia data also separate working-memory capacity, consolidation, maintenance, and interference resistance.

Therefore the cross-species construct must be **representational fidelity/stability**, not a commitment to one neural implementation.

## Cross-scale architecture surviving the run

The evidence converged on the following hierarchy:

`DISEASE / GENETIC / TRAUMATIC / VASCULAR / INFLAMMATORY / AGE-RELATED INPUT`

→ `ligand access and metabolic/trophic regulation`

→ `cell-specific synaptic and energetic homeostasis`

→ `E/I balance, receptor trafficking, spine/synapse integrity and adaptive myelination`

→ `PFC-centered distributed circuit coordination`

→ `fidelity of task-relevant representation under load/delay/interference`

→ `working-memory / executive-function performance`

→ `broader cognitive decline and disease-specific phenotype`

Missing links must remain unresolved rather than being filled by inference.

## Role of insulin/IGF after serial falsification

The run does **not** support a simple model in which globally reduced insulin signaling is the universal cause of cognitive decline.

The surviving interpretation is:

**Insulin/IGF signaling is a bona fide trophic, metabolic, synaptic, glial, and neurovascular regulatory system that can modulate the stability of executive-network representations. Disease-specific processes can disrupt this system, and the resulting dysregulation can in turn amplify synaptic/cognitive failure. The direction of the signaling change is pathway-, receptor-, cell-, region-, and stage-dependent.**

Key implications:

- INSR and IGF1R must be separated.
- IRS1 and IRS2 branches must be separated.
- neuronal, astrocytic, microglial, endothelial and pericyte effects must be separated.
- ligand delivery/BBB transport must be separated from intracellular receptor responsiveness.
- static pathway abundance is weaker evidence than dynamic response to insulin/IGF challenge.
- beneficial effects of reduced signaling in some models and harmful effects of receptor loss in others argue against a monotonic `more signaling = better cognition` rule.

## China-specific findings and implications

China was treated as a dedicated evidence family rather than a geographic afterthought.

High-information contributions identified in this run included:

1. Chinese schizophrenia cohorts linking serum IGF-1 and IGF-2 with executive/working-memory performance.
2. Chinese T2DM-MCI data linking the IGF-1/IGFBP-3 axis to Trail Making executive measures while a tested IGF-related polymorphism could be null.
3. Soochow University experimental work in which PFC neuronal IGFBP2 deficiency impaired synaptic structure/transmission and cognition, with local recombinant IGFBP2 rescue.
4. Chinese PFC working-memory physiology in rodents and primates, including delay-period activity and theta/low-gamma coupling.
5. Nanjing work showing mPFC activity-driven Wnt2-dependent adaptive myelination and working-memory recovery after chronic ischemic white-matter injury.
6. Chinese intranasal-insulin perioperative trials in which postoperative cognitive outcomes were improved in several studies, although a 2026 meta-analysis noted that the evidence was geographically concentrated and requires external replication.
7. Recent Beijing human prefrontal single-nucleus/spatial work showing stage- and cell-specific remodeling during AD progression.

## Highest-information experiment implied by the run

A decisive experiment should connect all levels in the same causal framework:

1. perturb insulin/IGF access or signaling in a defined disease/genetic model;
2. measure dynamic INSR/IGF1R and IRS1/IRS2 responses in a relevant PFC-centered region/cell type;
3. quantify synaptic/E-I/metabolic support;
4. measure representational fidelity during load/delay/interference at circuit level;
5. measure homologous working-memory/executive behavior;
6. rescue or reverse the signaling perturbation;
7. determine whether molecular, circuit and behavioral phenotypes recover in temporal sequence.

The strongest design would be isogenic or otherwise causally controlled and would include both a disease model and a non-disease perturbation model.

## What was pruned

The following formulations were pruned as insufficiently general:

- `Alzheimer disease = type 3 diabetes`;
- `low insulin signaling is the common cause of cognitive decline`;
- `persistent PFC firing is the universal working-memory mechanism`;
- `theta-gamma coupling is the universal biomarker`;
- `neuronal insulin resistance alone explains the phenotype`;
- `global cognition is the best transdiagnostic outcome`.

They remain branch-specific hypotheses where appropriate but are not the common seed.

## Surviving hypothesis

### H1 — Representation-stability / trophic-homeostasis model

Different diseases converge on impaired ability of PFC-centered distributed networks to maintain a high-fidelity task-relevant representation under load, delay and interference.

Insulin/IGF signaling is one major regulator of the cellular, synaptic, metabolic and neurovascular homeostasis supporting that state, but it is neither the sole regulator nor uniformly reduced.

### H2 — Disease-specific entry / common downstream phenotype

Genetic, proteinopathic, traumatic, vascular, inflammatory and metabolic disorders enter through different upstream mechanisms but can converge on the same executive-network failure phenotype.

### H3 — Feedback-amplification model

Primary disease processes can induce insulin/IGF dysregulation; dysregulated trophic/metabolic signaling can then amplify network instability and cognitive decline. Causality is therefore potentially bidirectional and stage-dependent.

These hypotheses are compatible and should be tested jointly rather than forced into a single exclusive explanation.
