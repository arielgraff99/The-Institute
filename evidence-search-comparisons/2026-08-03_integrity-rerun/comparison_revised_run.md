# Controlled comparison: original search versus integrity-integrated rerun

Protocol version 2.0. Search date 3 August 2026. Review type: controlled pilot rerun of a systematic-style evidence map. The original eligibility question and competing disease models were retained. This rerun added study-family linkage, result-level appraisal, registry and missing-evidence surveillance, explicit claim blocking, and dynamic search redistribution.

## Bottom line

The revised architecture does not reverse the earlier conclusion. It makes that conclusion more defensible and more conditional.

TRD remains a clinically important and regulator-recognized treatment-course population, but current evidence does not establish it as one biologically distinct disease separate from MDD. The best-supported explanation remains a mixed model: severity or stage, multiple possible endotypes, and operational or pseudo-resistance mechanisms contribute in different proportions.

The integrity layer especially changes how biomarker evidence is described. Genetics supports quantitative inherited enrichment rather than categorical separation. The 2025 blood transcriptomic signal remains promising discovery evidence, but a cross-sectional, treatment-exposed result without independent prospective validation cannot establish a disease boundary. Inflammation and imaging findings remain plausible subgroup signals with limited specificity and heterogeneous methods.

## What changed

| Dimension | Original run | Integrity-integrated rerun | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unit of evidence | Mostly publication | Result within linked study family | Reduces duplicate confirmation |
| Risk of bias | Qualitative, study-level | Design-routed, result-level | Exploratory biomarker findings receive less taxonomic weight |
| Replication | Discussed narratively | Independent cohort, dataset, laboratory, and pipeline required | Repeated analysis of one cohort cannot create consensus |
| File drawer | Limitation | Parallel registry/protocol/missing-evidence stream | Confidence is conditional on unreported and selectively reported evidence |
| Regulatory evidence | Interpreted cautiously | Explicit claim block against ontologic inference | TRD indication remains a treatment population, not a separate disease |
| Causal claims | Narrative restraint | Formal block when based only on association | Cross-sectional differences cannot be called causal mechanisms |
| Search adaptation | Sequential targeted audit | 60/25/15 dynamic redistribution | More effort goes to pretreatment prediction and replication |
| Output | One overall judgment | Multidimensional model profile plus blocked claims | Uncertainty becomes auditable |

## Claim audit

### Claim retained

TRD has greater clinical burden and a distinct later-line treatment pathway. This is directly supported, but it is not specific evidence of a distinct etiology.

### Claim reframed

Severe TRD carries greater inherited liability. The 2025 Nordic analysis reported genetic enrichment, but the very high genetic correlation with MDD and bipolar disorder supports quantitative and cross-diagnostic liability more than a clean boundary. The exact result is linked to the published primary report: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41380-025-03084-z

### Claim blocked from disease-level promotion

The blood transcriptomic study identifies a distinct molecular disease. Reason: the indexed 2025 report is discovery-stage and requires independent, pretreatment, prospective replication with control of medication exposure, illness duration, cell composition, and batch effects. Record: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40930269/

### Claim requiring additional missing-evidence work

The visible biomarker literature is representative of all completed biomarker research. This cannot be assumed. Relevant prospective and biomarker studies are registered, including NCT04342299, NCT06369792, NCT06337539, and NCT03096886; registry status, posted results, linked publications, outcome changes, and nonpublication require study-by-study verification before a formal publication-bias judgment.

## Search redistribution produced by the revised architecture

After reconnaissance, additional effort should move away from accumulating cross-sectional TRD-versus-MDD case-control differences and toward:

1. Treatment-naive or early-episode cohorts that predict later resistance.
2. External replication of transcriptomic, imaging, immune, and genetic classifiers.
3. Biomarker-by-treatment interactions rather than within-group response.
4. Registry-to-publication matching for completed depression biomarker studies.
5. Sensitivity analyses showing how TRD membership changes with adherence, psychotherapy, dose, duration, bipolar spectrum, medical comorbidity, and access.

## Highest-information future study

Recruit a treatment-naive, first-episode MDD cohort before resistance exists; collect harmonized multiomic, imaging, physiological, cognitive, sleep, clinical, pharmacokinetic, adherence, and socioeconomic measures; provide prospectively adequate measurement-based treatments; and freeze candidate subgroups before external validation. The decisive evidence would be a stable baseline subgroup that predicts natural history and a replicated subgroup-by-treatment interaction beyond severity, chronicity, diagnosis, adherence, and access.

## Process comparison

This pilot reused the earlier evidence map and added targeted live searches for prospective predictors, biomarker studies, registries, current genetics, transcriptomics, definitions, and publication-bias signals. Exact database screening totals, token counts, and costs from the earlier run were not recoverable and are therefore not invented. A production execution must collect them automatically using the fields in the JSON configuration.

## Limitations

- This is a controlled pilot rerun, not an exhaustive systematic review.
- Search coverage was limited to web-accessible PubMed-indexed, publisher, regulatory, and ClinicalTrials.gov materials.
- No licensed Embase, PsycINFO, or Web of Science search was performed.
- No duplicate human screening, PRESS review, or quantitative meta-analysis was performed.
- Registry matching was sampled to demonstrate the architecture; it was not completed for every eligible study.
- The conclusion is a methodological synthesis from cited evidence, not a validated diagnostic algorithm.
