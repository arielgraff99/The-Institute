# Report 1: Simulated AI Co-Scientist Run

Delusion versus confabulation when fragmented memories are suspected
Rapid evidence synthesis and methodological simulation | Search date: July 31, 2026

## Executive result

The tournament selected a mixed sequential model as the most research-productive hypothesis: degraded episodic traces and impaired reality filtering generate an inaccurate event representation; threat salience and defective belief updating then stabilize that representation as a delusion-like conviction. This model ranked above pure confabulation because it explains persistent persecutory meaning, and above pure delusion because it predicts source and temporal errors. It remains a hypothesis, not a validated diagnostic rule.

Important limitation: this is a transparent simulation based on Google's published Generation, Proximity, Reflection, Ranking, Evolution and Meta-review architecture. It did not access or reproduce Google's proprietary Co-Scientist service.

## Research objective supplied to the system

Determine whether confident false accounts in people with fragmented memory arise from confabulation, delusional interpretation, or interaction between the two, and identify observations or tests that distinguish these mechanisms.

## Generation phase: candidate hypotheses

| ID | Hypothesis | Core mechanism | Distinctive prediction |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Primary psychotic attribution | A substantially preserved event is assigned abnormal agency, intention or causal meaning. | Stable theme; reasoning biases; neutral memory may be relatively intact. |
| H2 | Fragmented-trace reconstruction | Weak episodic traces are completed with gist, habits and semantic fragments. | Errors cluster in memory gaps; misplaced real fragments; cue-dependent intrusions. |
| H3 | Reality-filter failure | Activated memories or imaginings are not rejected when irrelevant to current reality. | False-positive recognition; imagined-as-real errors; behavior guided by obsolete memories. |
| H4 | Sequential mixed model | H2/H3 generates an inaccurate event; abnormal salience and belief updating stabilize threat meaning. | Changing factual details with stable persecutory interpretation. |
| H5 | State-dependent common cause | Delirium, seizures, sleep disruption, medication toxicity or affective activation impairs memory and reality testing. | Parallel fluctuation with physiological state. |
| H6 | Interview/collateral artefact | Leading questions, repeated rehearsal, suggestibility or incorrect collateral creates apparent pathology. | Errors depend on interviewer/source and do not generalize to neutral tasks. |

## Proximity phase: conceptual clusters

- Memory-construction cluster: H2 and H3 share impaired contextual control but differ on whether the decisive problem is a degraded trace or failure to reject an activated trace.
- Belief-formation cluster: H1 emphasizes attribution, salience, jumping to conclusions and reduced response to disconfirming evidence.
- Interaction cluster: H4 links a memory-origin error to a second-stage belief-stabilization process.
- Alternative-cause cluster: H5 and H6 protect the tournament from falsely forcing a primary neurocognitive or psychotic explanation.

## Reflection phase: virtual peer-review reports

| Hypothesis | Reflection-agent critique |
|---|---|
| H1 | Strength: accounts for stable conviction and cross-situational threat interpretation. Weakness: does not predict why errors preferentially occupy objectively demonstrated memory gaps or why real fragments migrate across time/source. |
| H2 | Strength: supported by habit-for-episode substitutions and temporal/source errors. Weakness: severe amnesia is neither necessary nor sufficient; filling gaps is too nonspecific without monitoring errors. |
| H3 | Strength: experimentally linked to false alarms, indiscriminate confidence and failure to suppress currently irrelevant memories. Weakness: most evidence comes from small lesion cohorts and does not by itself explain persecutory organization. |
| H4 | Strength: explains simultaneous factual migration and thematic stability. Weakness: risks becoming unfalsifiably broad unless the two stages are measured independently and temporally. |
| H5 | Strength: clinically essential and temporally testable. Weakness: becomes weak if the phenomenon remains stable after physiological normalization. |
| H6 | Strength: necessary null/artefact control. Weakness: cannot explain generalized source-monitoring errors or spontaneous behavior based on false memories. |

Ranking interpretation: Co-Scientist-style tournaments reward novelty, mechanistic integration and experimental productivity. The rank is not a posterior probability and should not be used as a diagnosis.

## Evolution phase: refined leading hypothesis

Stage A - trace vulnerability: incomplete encoding, accelerated forgetting or impaired binding reduces event-specific information while preserving gist, habits or emotionally salient fragments.

Stage B - contextual acceptance: source, temporal or reality-monitoring failure permits a familiar, imagined or displaced fragment to be accepted as the target event.

Stage C - salience and attribution: threatening or self-relevant meaning receives disproportionate weight, producing an intentional or persecutory interpretation.

Stage D - stabilization: jumping to conclusions and reduced integration of disconfirming evidence preserve the explanatory theme even when factual details migrate.

## Predictions and proposed experiment

| Domain | Pure confabulation | Pure delusion | Evolved mixed model |
|---|---|---|---|
| Verified neutral events | Confident intrusions and context errors | No necessary deficit | Intrusions/context errors present |
| Repeated account | Material details migrate | Core factual account and meaning relatively stable | Facts migrate; threat theme persists |
| Source/reality monitoring | Impaired | May be intact | Impaired |
| Belief updating | Not necessarily globally impaired | Reduced for disconfirming evidence | Reduced, especially after threat attribution |
| Physiological fluctuation | Only if secondary to a state | Possible but not required | Amplifies both stages |

## Highest-value study design

- Construct a set of independently verified recent events, similar events from another day, confirmed non-events and genuinely unknown details.
- Obtain free recall before cueing; code each proposition for accuracy, source, temporal location, confidence and recollection versus inference.
- Repeat with neutral wording after delay and quantify factual migration separately from thematic stability.
- Administer matched neutral source/reality-monitoring tasks and a psychosis battery measuring conviction, preoccupation and belief updating.
- Predefine predictions for H1-H6 and retain an artefact model; blind the primary coder to event truth status when feasible.

## Meta-review conclusion

The simulation generates a scientifically interesting mixed mechanism and a prospective protocol capable of testing it. Its main weakness is clinical calibration: a highly ranked hypothesis may be novel and testable without being the most probable explanation for an individual. Diagnostic use therefore requires the separate clinical model report.
