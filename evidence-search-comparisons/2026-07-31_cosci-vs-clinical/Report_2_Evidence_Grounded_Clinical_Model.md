# Report 2: Evidence-Grounded Clinical Model

Patient-level discrimination of delusion, confabulation, mixed mechanisms and artefact
Rapid evidence synthesis and methodological simulation | Search date: July 31, 2026

## Clinical conclusion

Fragmented memory increases the opportunity for reconstruction but does not establish confabulation. The probability of confabulation rises when objective episodic weakness converges with high-confidence intrusions, temporal/source displacement, imagined-as-real errors, reduced acknowledgement of uncertainty, and the same pattern on independent emotionally neutral events. Delusion is favored when memory for the underlying event is substantially preserved but agency, intention or causal meaning is pathologically inferred and remains thematically stable despite contrary evidence.

The most defensible outcome may be a mixed formulation. A confabulated event representation can become incorporated into a stable persecutory belief, or psychosis can distort interpretation while memory disease separately distorts event details.

## Competing clinical models retained

| Model | Necessary evidence | Evidence that weakens it | Clinical status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary confabulation | Verified memory gap plus confident intrusions/context errors that generalize beyond the disputed event. | Poor recall without intrusions; intact source/temporal monitoring; stable accurate facts. | Retain until demonstrated, not inferred from low MoCA. |
| Primary delusion | Pathological attribution or explanatory belief; thematic generalization; impaired belief updating; psychotic context. | Errors limited to missing memories and normalize when event facts are supplied. | Can coexist with neurocognitive disorder. |
| Mixed sequential | Independent evidence for both memory-origin error and delusional stabilization. | Only one mechanism is demonstrated. | Often clinically plausible; must not become a catch-all. |
| State-dependent | Temporal covariation with delirium, seizure, sleep, substance, medication or mood state. | Persistence after state normalization. | Rule out early because treatment changes. |
| Artefact/collateral error | Dependence on leading questions, rehearsal, misinformation or unreliable external records. | Generalized neutral-task errors or spontaneous behavior independent of interview. | Mandatory alternative, especially for allegations. |

## Operational assessment sequence

1. Stabilize and exclude state causes: assess delirium, intoxication/withdrawal, medication effects, sleep-wake disruption, seizures, acute neurological disease and affective activation.
2. Separate recollection from inference: for every proposition ask what is directly remembered, inferred, reported by someone else, or possibly imagined/dreamed/from another occasion.
3. Build a verified event set using contemporaneous records and lawful collateral, with confirmed events, confirmed non-events, similar events on other dates and unknowns.
4. Test free recall first. Record uninterrupted recall, then time, place, source, sequence and confidence for each material detail. Cueing and recognition come later.
5. Repeat neutrally after delay using different wording. Code propositions rather than global impressions.
6. Test generalization on emotionally neutral events.
7. Measure both domains: formal neuropsychology for memory/context monitoring plus dimensional assessment of delusions and belief updating.
8. Integrate rather than force a binary answer; document what would change the formulation.

## Proposition-level coding form

| Field | Coding options |
|---|---|
| Truth status | Verified correct; verified incorrect; genuinely unknown |
| Memory status | Direct recollection; inference; told by another; dream/imagination; unclear |
| Error type | Intrusion; temporal displacement; source error; person/place binding error; contradiction |
| Confidence | 0-100 for each proposition |
| Stability | Stable; elaborated; migrated; retracted; newly generated after cueing |
| Meaning | Neutral; threat; reference; guilt; grandiosity; somatic; other |
| Behavioral effect | No action; avoidance; accusation; help-seeking; aggression; other |

## Formal testing

### Memory and confabulation domain

- Episodic learning: CVLT, RAVLT or HVLT; WMS Logical Memory/Visual Reproduction; delayed visual recall. Examine learning slope, retention, intrusions, false positives and recognition discriminability.
- Source and context: who/where/when, imagined versus perceived, self- versus other-generated, temporal-order and list-discrimination paradigms.
- Autobiographical memory: event specificity, internal versus generic detail, temporal binding and collateral accuracy across life periods.
- Confabulation Battery for provoked confabulation; NVCL-20 for observed spontaneous/provoked behavior, with caution because validation is concentrated in Korsakoff/alcohol-related populations.
- Executive monitoring: inhibition, set shifting, working memory and error monitoring. Executive dysfunction supports a mechanism but is not specific.

### Delusion and psychosis domain

- Rate conviction, preoccupation, distress, behavioral influence, attribution of agency/intention, alternative explanations and response to contradictory evidence.
- Use PSYRATS delusion subscale, Brown Assessment of Beliefs Scale or another dimensional instrument appropriate to context; these characterize belief dimensions but do not independently distinguish confabulation.
- Performance tasks for jumping to conclusions and bias against disconfirmatory evidence may support a reasoning mechanism; clinical diagnostic specificity is limited.

## Discriminating interpretation matrix

| Finding | Probability shift |
|---|---|
| Poor recall only | Toward amnesia; insufficient for confabulation. |
| High-confidence intrusions where recall is weak | Toward confabulation, especially if replicated on neutral events. |
| Real fragments assigned to wrong dates, sources or people | Toward contextual-memory confabulation. |
| Material facts migrate but a persecutory theme persists | Toward a mixed model. |
| Facts remain accurate; implausible intention/causality persists | Toward delusional interpretation. |
| Symptoms fluctuate with attention, arousal, sleep or medication exposure | Toward a state-dependent common cause. |
| Errors appear only after leading questions or misinformation | Toward interview/suggestibility artefact. |

## Treatment implications

The mechanism should guide treatment, but the relationship is not binary. Antipsychotics may reduce coexisting psychosis, affective activation, agitation or risk without correcting a memory-monitoring disorder. Confabulation management emphasizes treatment of the cause, environmental structure, non-confrontational correction, cueing, error reduction and rehabilitation. Delirium, seizure, medication toxicity, sleep disruption and neurodegenerative disease require etiologic treatment. A time-limited antipsychotic trial cannot by itself validate a delusional mechanism because nonspecific behavioral improvement is possible.

## Documentation language

Preferred wording: The account contains verified inaccuracies. Confabulation is suspected because errors occur in objectively demonstrated memory gaps and include repeated high-confidence temporal/source intrusions on independent neutral events. A delusional contribution remains possible because the persecutory interpretation is stable despite migration of factual details.

Avoid inferring confabulation simply because a story is improbable and the patient has cognitive impairment. Probability, cognitive screening and disagreement with collateral do not establish the mechanism or truth status of a specific allegation.

## Evidence limits

Direct head-to-head diagnostic-accuracy studies of delusion versus confabulation are scarce. Evidence is assembled across lesion cohorts, Korsakoff syndrome, dementia, schizophrenia and experimental cognitive-bias studies. Most proposed tests are mechanism-supporting rather than validated differential diagnostic tests. This report is a rapid evidence synthesis, not a systematic review or a patient-specific diagnosis.
