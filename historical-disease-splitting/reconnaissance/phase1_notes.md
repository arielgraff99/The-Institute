# Phase 1 Historical Reconnaissance Notes

Run date: 2026-08-04

Status: exploratory seed construction only. No disease-splitting criteria are frozen.

## Objective

Build a diverse set of historical natural experiments in which medicine either successfully divided a previously unified disease concept, created a clinically useful subtype/stage/endotype, or proposed a split that later weakened or failed. Reconstruct scientific and contextual causes separately.

## Initial successful/comparator set

### Type 1 versus Type 2 diabetes

The 1979 National Diabetes Data Group explicitly described diabetes as a heterogeneous syndrome and proposed a classification reflecting contemporary knowledge rather than older age-of-onset labels. Later etiologic understanding strengthened separation. This is a useful example in which nomenclature evolved as disease mechanism became clearer.

### Crohn disease versus ulcerative colitis

The 1932 Crohn/Ginzburg/Oppenheimer report described regional ileitis as a pathologic and clinical entity with symptoms resembling ulcerative colitis but distinctive anatomic and tissue features. Subsequent reports extended the recognized distribution. This is a classic phenotype/pathology-led split in which molecular mechanism was not required at the moment of recognition.

### Viral hepatitis

The hepatitis history is a high-value contextual case. World War II outbreaks created direct military urgency. Military investigation helped establish that clinically similar hepatitis syndromes included different forms, and later virology/serology separated hepatitis A, B, non-A/non-B and other viral entities. This case demonstrates that war can accelerate rather than simply suppress disease differentiation when the condition becomes strategically important.

### Acute promyelocytic leukemia

APL is a high-value case because the chronology is unusually informative. Distinctive clinicopathology came first; cytogenetics and PML-RARA later provided strong molecular coherence; ATRA/ATO produced dramatic treatment specificity. Chinese ATRA/ATO discoveries also show that major scientific advances can emerge in adverse political-economic conditions. ATO was rediscovered during the Cultural Revolution, and historical accounts note that the original Chinese work was poorly recognized in English literature for years. ATRA clinical differentiation therapy preceded knowledge of the disease-specific retinoic-acid receptor fusion. This case directly challenges any rule requiring molecular mechanism to precede a valid clinical subdivision.

### HER2-positive breast cancer

HER2 amplification/overexpression was linked to aggressive clinical behavior, then became a target for trastuzumab. Publicly supported molecular research, biotechnology capability, industry development, a companion diagnostic, regulatory approval, and patient advocacy all contributed to translation. This case is useful for separating biological validity from the economic/institutional machinery that made the subtype clinically actionable.

### Asthma T2-high/T2-low endotypes

Asthma illustrates transition from broad overlapping phenotypes toward mechanism-linked endotypes. Modern reviews attribute this shift to omics, large cohorts, improved statistical methods and targeted biologic trials. This is useful because the endpoint is not necessarily 'two separate diseases'; the historically derived framework must be able to identify a mechanistic endotype without overpromoting it to distinct-disease status.

### Multiple sclerosis clinical phenotypes

The 1996 MS clinical-course descriptors were created partly to standardize communication and trial recruitment. The 2013 revision explicitly noted that imaging and biological markers sufficient to objectively separate phenotypes were lacking. This is a critical comparator showing that a useful disease-stage/phenotype classification can be scientifically legitimate without representing separate diseases.

## Initial negative/transition controls

### Traditional schizophrenia subtypes

DSM-IV schizophrenia subtypes were removed in DSM-5 because of poor longitudinal stability, reliability and validity, and because they did not show distinctive treatment response or longitudinal course. This provides a long-lived classification that failed to retain scientific utility.

### Renin-defined essential hypertension

Low/normal/high-renin hypertension provides a valuable non-psychiatric negative control. The subdivision had attractive mechanistic, prognostic and treatment promises, but classification was highly sensitive to diet, posture and analytic conditions. Contemporary critique reported that etiologic, prognostic and therapeutic promises had not held up. This may become particularly useful for testing candidate criteria related to biomarker stability, measurement invariance and treatment utility.

### Intrinsic/extrinsic asthma

The older binary asthma phenotype is useful as an internal transition control. It captured some real heterogeneity but overlapped substantially and was later superseded by more mechanistically grounded classifications.

### Sepsis/SIRS/severe-sepsis categories

Useful as an operational classification control. Repeated consensus revisions and the removal of SIRS from the core Sepsis-3 definition illustrate that clinically useful thresholds and categories may evolve without corresponding to distinct disease ontology.

## Early methodological observations — NOT YET CRITERIA

The cases already warn against several simplistic rules:

1. Molecular mechanism does not need to be known before a valid split is recognized: Crohn disease and APL are examples.
2. Treatment specificity can strongly validate a split but may arise before full mechanistic understanding: APL is especially informative.
3. A classification can be useful without denoting distinct diseases: MS clinical phenotypes and sepsis categories illustrate this.
4. A plausible biomarker can fail as a stable classifier if it is context/measurement sensitive: renin-defined hypertension is a strong control.
5. Historical delay does not equal weak biology: APL/ATO recognition was strongly shaped by political and scientific communication conditions.
6. War and political context can suppress some science but accelerate other disease distinctions if strategic need redirects resources: viral hepatitis is a clear example.
7. Commercial and regulatory translation can amplify a biologically coherent subtype but should not be mistaken for the biological evidence itself: HER2 is a useful comparator.
8. Repeated use in diagnostic manuals does not establish validity: schizophrenia subtypes demonstrate classification inertia.

These observations nominate questions for later cross-case testing. They are not promoted to CORE_CRITERIA until the historical sample is expanded and candidate criteria undergo face-validity and successful-versus-failed-split comparison.

## Next historical expansion

The next cycle should broaden the sample in at least these directions:

- infectious disease etiologic splits beyond hepatitis;
- neurodegenerative disease boundaries with substantial mixed pathology;
- autoimmune diseases where biomarkers changed classification;
- cancer subtypes with both successful and unsuccessful molecular stratification;
- diseases in low-resource or war-disrupted settings;
- historical splits originating outside North America/Western Europe;
- classifications heavily shaped by reimbursement or regulatory incentives;
- historical cases where an initially accepted split was later merged again.

The goal is sampling diversity, not citation volume.
