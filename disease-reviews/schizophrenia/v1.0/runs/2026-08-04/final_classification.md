# Schizophrenia Subtype Review Module v1.0

Run date: 2026-08-04  
Historical ruler: frozen Historical Disease-Splitting Framework v0.2  
Target-family holdout: excludes HIST008 and HIST035; stricter non-psychiatric sensitivity also excludes HIST019.  
Scope: executable sentinel evidence-map run; not a licensed-database systematic review.

## Primary result

**Preferred representation: COMPOSITE_MULTIAXIAL / HETEROGENEOUS_SYNDROME.**

The evidence does not support schizophrenia as a set of stable DSM-style clinical subtypes, nor does it support one single biological subdivision that explains the syndrome. Instead, several partly orthogonal structures coexist:

1. **Treatment-response state:** treatment-resistant schizophrenia (TRS) is the strongest operational subdivision. It is clinically reproducible when adequate exposure and adherence are established, and it has a distinctive clozapine treatment pathway. It is not established as one distinct disease.
2. **Nested treatment-response state:** clozapine-resistant schizophrenia is a clinically meaningful later resistance state but has insufficient biological validation for separate ontology.
3. **Negative-symptom/deficit phenotype:** primary enduring negative symptoms identify a clinically important subgroup, but secondary negative symptoms and dimensional variation prevent promotion to a separate disease.
4. **Cognitive subtypes:** normal/intermediate/impaired cognitive profiles replicate and have neuroanatomical correlates, but current evidence better fits quantitative dimensions/phenotypes than discrete diseases.
5. **Psychosis Biotypes:** B-SNIP provides the strongest evidence for reproducible biological subgrouping, including replication, temporal stability, imaging validation, and familial signal. Crucially, these Biotypes cross schizophrenia, schizoaffective disorder and psychotic bipolar disorder. They are therefore better interpreted as **transdiagnostic psychosis endotypes** than schizophrenia-specific diseases.
6. **Immune/inflammatory endotypes:** response-stratified immune profiles are promising but remain provisional; current classifiers lack independent prospective validation and proven treatment utility.
7. **Neuroimaging-only subtypes:** not yet sufficiently reproducible. A 2026 critical appraisal found no consistently reproduced macroscale imaging subtype and suggested that many clusters may reflect continuous variation.

## Historical-gate interpretation

### Distinct disease
Fails boundary robustness and intrinsic-discriminator gates. Schizophrenia genetics is highly polygenic and overlaps with other psychiatric disorders; no stable set of etiologic diseases currently maps onto clinical subtype boundaries.

### Biological subtype/endotype
Supported only for **candidate cross-cutting endotypes**, not as a single global subdivision of schizophrenia. B-SNIP is the strongest example, but it is explicitly transdiagnostic.

### Dimension/spectrum
Strongly supported. Positive, negative, disorganized, cognitive, mood and motor manifestations vary continuously and partly independently.

### Disease stage
Partially supported. Early-course, chronicity and deterioration/recovery trajectories matter, but no single universal stage system captures all patients.

### Treatment-response state
Supported for TRS when treatment adequacy and adherence are verified. Pseudoresistance remains an essential control.

### Operational clinical category
Supported. TRS and persistent negative-symptom constructs can improve communication, treatment selection and trial design without implying separate diseases.

## Target-family holdout

Removing schizophrenia-subtype and Kraepelinian psychosis precedents from the historical support does not change the classification. The result therefore does not depend on using schizophrenia's own failed historical categories to judge schizophrenia.

## Current formulation

Schizophrenia is best represented as a **heritable, multidimensional syndrome containing cross-cutting biological endotypes/biotypes, cognitive and negative-symptom dimensions, variable longitudinal trajectories, and treatment-response states**. The most biologically persuasive subgroup evidence currently cuts across conventional schizophrenia boundaries rather than dividing schizophrenia neatly from within.

## Highest-information future study

Enroll first-episode, minimally treated psychosis before treatment-response status is known; collect harmonized genetics, immune/metabolic measures, PET/MRS, structural/functional imaging, EEG/ERP, cognition and detailed environmental/context data; standardize sequential antipsychotic exposure with objective adherence; freeze candidate biotypes before external validation; and test whether baseline subtypes predict natural history and differential treatment benefit across independent settings.

## Limitations

This is a first executable schizophrenia subtype module using 32 sentinel records from multiple evidence families. It is broad but not exhaustive, and no study-family-reconstructed meta-analysis was performed.
