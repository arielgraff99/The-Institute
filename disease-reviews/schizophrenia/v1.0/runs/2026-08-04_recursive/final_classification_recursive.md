# Schizophrenia Subtype Review Module v1.0 — Recursive Final Classification

Run date: 2026-08-04
Historical ruler: frozen Historical Disease-Splitting Framework v0.2
Scope: recursively expanded web-accessible evidence map with study-family audit and saturation stopping; not a licensed-database systematic review.

## Primary result

**Preferred representation: COMPOSITE_MULTIAXIAL / HETEROGENEOUS_SYNDROME.**

The recursive run does not support a return to traditional symptom-based schizophrenia subtypes and does not identify one mutually exclusive biological subtype system that explains schizophrenia. Instead, several structures coexist at different levels.

## 1. Rare etiologic diseases can sit underneath a schizophrenia-spectrum presentation

The strongest new refinement is that some patients presenting with a schizophrenia-spectrum phenotype have demonstrable etiologic diagnoses. Examples include 22q11.2 deletion syndrome and GRIN2A-null-related disorder; autoimmune encephalitis/autoimmune psychosis can also produce schizophrenia-like psychosis in selected patients.

These should not be interpreted as new paranoid/disorganized-style schizophrenia subtypes. They are better classified as etiologically defined genetic, neurodevelopmental or immune diseases that can manifest psychosis.

Status: **RARE_ETIOLOGICAL_DISEASE_SUBGROUPS_PRESENT — SUPPORTED AS EXCEPTIONS.**

## 2. The strongest reproducible biological subgrouping remains transdiagnostic

B-SNIP psychosis Biotypes remain the strongest replicated biomarker-defined grouping in the evidence map. Discovery/replication samples show stable cognitive, oculomotor and EEG/ERP profiles; imaging, familial and genetic analyses provide additional construct validation.

The important limitation is also the scientific result: the Biotypes cross schizophrenia, schizoaffective disorder and bipolar disorder with psychosis. They therefore fit **transdiagnostic psychosis endotypes** better than schizophrenia-specific diseases.

Status: **TRANSDIAGNOSTIC_PSYCHOSIS_ENDOTYPES — STRONGLY PROVISIONAL.**

They are not yet decision-grade because prospective evidence that Biotype-guided treatment improves outcome is unavailable.

## 3. Schizophrenia-internal cognitive subtypes became more credible

The 2026 EF-Focal/EF-Global study identified two executive-function profiles with longitudinal stability, different symptoms and immune-metabolic correlates, local independent validation (subtype classification AUC 0.79), and prediction of remission independent of baseline severity (AUC 0.78).

This upgrades cognitive subtyping from a purely dimensional observation to a **provisional biological-clinical subtype**. It still fails promotion to a distinct disease because geographic external replication, boundary robustness in ambiguous cases and treatment-by-subtype utility are not established.

Status: **SCHIZOPHRENIA_INTERNAL_COGNITIVE_SUBTYPES — PROVISIONALLY SUPPORTED.**

## 4. TRS is a robust treatment-response state, not an established separate disease

TRRIP-style criteria make TRS clinically reproducible when adequate antipsychotic exposure and adherence are established, and clozapine creates a distinctive treatment pathway. TRS can emerge early, arguing against a model in which all resistance is simply accumulated chronic deterioration.

Prospective biology is improving. BIOERES followed 44 first-episode schizophrenia-spectrum patients for five years; lower baseline CSF LDH plus early antipsychotic response produced an exploratory AUC of 0.86 for future TRS. The authors explicitly call for independent validation.

Dopamine, glutamate, immune and genetic studies suggest several routes to resistance rather than one universal TRS mechanism.

Status: **TRS_TREATMENT_RESPONSE_STATE — SUPPORTED.**

Status as one distinct biological disease: **NOT ESTABLISHED.**

## 5. Negative-symptom/deficit phenotypes remain clinically important but ontologically incomplete

Primary enduring negative symptoms and deficit-syndrome constructs show reproducible clinical, cognitive and neural differences. However, depression, positive symptoms, extrapyramidal effects, sedation, deprivation and other factors can generate secondary negative symptoms, and symptom severity remains substantially dimensional.

Status: **DEFICIT/PRIMARY-NEGATIVE PHENOTYPE — PARTIALLY SUPPORTED AS PHENOTYPE/DIMENSION.**

## 6. Neuroimaging subtyping is genuinely conflicted

Large cross-site work using SuStaIn provides positive evidence for cortical-predominant and subcortical-predominant neurostructural trajectories. In contrast, a 2026 critical review of clustering studies found no macroscale imaging subtype profile that had been consistently reproduced across the field and warned that clusters can represent discretized continuous variation.

The conflict is retained rather than averaged away.

Status: **NEUROIMAGING SUBTYPES — CONTESTED / PROVISIONAL.**

## 7. Immune/inflammatory endotypes remain provisional

TRS and response-stratified cohorts show recurring inflammatory and immune-cell signals. However, much of this evidence is measured after substantial antipsychotic exposure, and clozapine itself can alter immune biology. A pretreatment, externally validated, treatment-modifying immune classifier remains missing.

Status: **IMMUNE/INFLAMMATORY ENDOTYPES — PROVISIONAL.**

## 8. Common-variant genetics weakens a clean schizophrenia boundary

Large cross-disorder genomic analysis of more than one million cases found extensive shared psychiatric genetic structure. Schizophrenia and bipolar disorder form a highly overlapping genomic factor with relatively few disorder-specific loci. This does not erase clinically useful diagnoses, but it argues against expecting common-variant genetics to divide schizophrenia neatly into isolated biological diseases matching current diagnostic boundaries.

## Historical-framework adjudication

### DISTINCT_DISEASE
A global subdivision of schizophrenia into a small number of separate diseases is **NOT SUPPORTED**. Rare etiologic exceptions do meet disease-like logic individually, but they are too heterogeneous and uncommon to constitute a complete schizophrenia taxonomy.

### BIOLOGICAL_SUBTYPE_OR_ENDOTYPE
**PARTIALLY/PROVISIONALLY SUPPORTED**, especially for transdiagnostic B-SNIP Biotypes and emerging cognitive/immune subgroups. No single endotype partition captures the whole schizophrenia population.

### DIMENSION_OR_SPECTRUM
**SUPPORTED.** Positive, negative, disorganization, cognition, mood, motor and functional dimensions vary partly independently and often continuously.

### DISEASE_STAGE
**PARTIALLY SUPPORTED.** Early-course, chronic and recovery/deterioration trajectories are meaningful, but no single stage system captures all patients.

### TREATMENT_RESPONSE_STATE
**SUPPORTED for TRS** when exposure and pseudoresistance are controlled.

### OPERATIONAL_CLINICAL_CATEGORY
**SUPPORTED** for treatment and research constructs such as TRS and persistent negative-symptom populations, without implying separate disease ontology.

## Holdout sensitivity

Removing the historical schizophrenia-subtype case and Kraepelinian psychosis boundary from the criteria-support set does not change the result. The stricter non-psychiatric holdout also excludes the autism/PDD merger and remains compatible with the same classification.

## Saturation

The last material refinement occurred in cycle 7, when 2026 executive-function evidence strengthened the schizophrenia-internal cognitive-subtype branch. Cycles 8, 9 and 10 added replication checks, contradictory evidence and prospective-utility gaps but no class-level change. The prespecified requirement of three consecutive no-change cycles was therefore met.

## Final model

A useful representation is:

**etiologic exceptions**
+ **transdiagnostic biological psychosis endotypes**
+ **schizophrenia-internal cognitive/negative-symptom phenotypes and dimensions**
+ **longitudinal trajectories**
+ **treatment-response states such as TRS**
= **the current schizophrenia syndrome**.

The key scientific implication is that the most biologically coherent subdivisions often either cross the schizophrenia boundary or identify a specific etiology beneath it. Biology does not currently recreate the traditional clinical subtype tree.

## Highest-information next study

Recruit a large, minimally treated first-episode psychosis cohort before treatment-response status is known; perform standardized genomic rare-variant/CNV evaluation, targeted autoimmune workup when clinically indicated, harmonized immune/metabolic assays, PET/MRS, MRI, EEG/ERP and cognition; administer standardized sequential antipsychotic treatment with objective adherence; freeze candidate biotypes/subtypes before external validation; and test both natural history and treatment-by-subtype interactions in geographically independent cohorts.
